The headline

The technical foundation is genuinely good — better than most sites at this stage. What's missing is everything that makes Google understand you are a Tamil Nadu business selling a product at a price. That gap is where all the revenue is.

You are invisible for the searches that convert. Not because of a penalty or a crawl problem, but because nothing on the site says where you are or what things cost. A person in Coimbatore searching “nfc business card near me” has no way to reach you, and Google has no signal to serve you.

There is also one active defect that will fail Google's Rich Results Test today, and one claim on the homepage that is a genuine liability. Both are quick fixes.

Critical

Fix these first. Items 1–3 are the difference between ranking in Tamil Nadu and not existing there.

CriticalNo LocalBusiness schema — you cannot enter the local pack

The site emits Organization, which tells Google you're a company. It does not emit LocalBusiness, which tells Google you're a company with a physical location that serves a geographic area. Those are different eligibility paths, and only the second one feeds the map pack.

For a Trichy-based business whose entire realistic market is Tamil Nadu, this is the single most valuable missing tag on the site.

FixAdd LocalBusiness (or Store) with areaServed: "Tamil Nadu", geo coordinates, openingHoursSpecification and priceRange. Emit it on the homepage and /contact-us only — not sitewide.

CriticalProduct schema states prices that do not exist

Your global JSON-LD hardcodes an AggregateOffer. I compared it against the live database. It is wrong in three fields at once:

Schema claims Database reality ───────────────────── ────────────────────────── lowPrice 599 ₹599 Elegant & Professional highPrice 799 ₹999 black card offerCount 6 2 active products

Publishing a highPrice of ₹799 when your actual top product is ₹999 is a price mismatch. Google treats structured data that contradicts the visible page as a manual-action risk, and it will fail validation regardless.

FixGenerate the offer block from the same query /cards already uses, so the numbers cannot drift. If pricing is dynamic, it must be derived, never typed.

CriticalProduct schema is emitted on every page, including Contact and Privacy

The <JsonLd /> component sits in the root layout, so all 24 pages declare the same Product and AggregateOffer — your privacy policy currently tells Google it is a purchasable NFC card priced from ₹599.

Product markup on a non-product page is invalid structured data. Combined with the wrong prices above, this is the highest-risk item on the site.

FixKeep Organization and WebSite sitewide — those are correct there. Move Product to /cards and to per-card pages once they exist.

CriticalUnverifiable rating and customer-count claims on the homepage

Two claims sit in the hero at HeroSection.tsx:98–99:

4.9/5 Trusted by 10,000+ professionals

The database holds 2 products and no review table. These figures cannot be substantiated. Beyond the Google risk, unsubstantiated superiority claims are actionable under the Consumer Protection Act 2019 — and India's ASCI has pursued exactly this pattern.

One thing was done right: no aggregateRating appears in your JSON-LD. That is the difference between a copy problem and a structured-data manual action. Do not let a future change wire this figure into schema.

FixReplace with facts you can defend — cards shipped, institutions served, cities delivered to, year founded — and render each only when it has a real value.

CriticalZero location targeting anywhere on the site

I searched every page for Tamil Nadu city names. Outside a shipping-policy line and a map caption, there is nothing. No page title, heading or description contains “Tamil Nadu”, “Chennai”, “Coimbatore”, “Madurai” or “Trichy”.

In this category Indian buyers search locally and commercially — “nfc card chennai”, “visiting card coimbatore”, “nfc business card price india”. You currently rank for none of that shape of query because you have no page addressing it.

FixCity landing pages, each with genuinely local content — Tirupur knitwear exporters, Sivakasi printing, Trichy education. Templated pages with the city name swapped get flagged as doorway pages, so uniqueness matters more than page count.

The Tamil Nadu opportunity

This is where the growth is, and almost none of it is a code problem. Two of the three biggest levers sit outside the repository entirely.

1. Google Business Profile — do this before anything in this document

You are a physical business in Tiruchirappalli. For “near me” and city-qualified searches, GBP is the dominant ranking factor, ahead of everything on your website. Without a verified profile you cannot appear in the map pack at all, no matter how good the schema is.

Verify the listing, categorise it as Business card printing service or Digital printer, add real photos of the cards, and start collecting genuine reviews. Real reviews also solve the 4.9/5 problem honestly — at which point aggregateRating becomes legitimately available to you.

2. NAP consistency — your address is incomplete everywhere

Name, Address, Phone must match character-for-character across your site, GBP and every directory. Yours is currently city-level only:

Present Tiruchirappalli, Tamil Nadu, India Missing streetAddress, postalCode, geo coordinates

Google cannot confidently place a business without a street address and PIN code. Add both to site-config.ts, then build citations on Justdial, IndiaMART and Sulekha — these carry real weight for Indian local search and are how B2B buyers here actually find suppliers.

3. Tamil language — a genuinely open field

The site is lang="en" with no hreflang. Tamil-language commercial search has a fraction of the competition of English, and your buyers — shop owners, clinics, small manufacturers — search in Tamil.

Terms worth owning: என்.எஃப்.சி கார்டு, டிஜிட்டல் விசிட்டிங் கார்டு, என்.எஃப்.சி விசிட்டிங் கார்டு விலை.

Do not machine-translate. Poor Tamil reads as a scam to a native speaker and will cost you more trust than the traffic is worth. Commission real copy for a handful of pages rather than auto-generating the whole site.

4. The product category you are missing entirely

Google Review NFC cards — a card a customer taps to leave a Google review — are the highest-converting NFC product in the Indian market right now. Every competitor sells them. You have no page for it. It also sells to exactly the buyers you can reach locally: restaurants, salons, clinics, workshops.

What is already working

Worth stating plainly, because several of these are things sites at this stage usually get wrong.

SolidDomain and canonical architecture

One constant drives every URL on the site. All 24 pages emit correct canonicals and og:url on tapvyo.in, with no stray references to the old host. Most migrations leak; this one doesn't.

SolidSitemap and robots are generated, not hand-maintained

20 URLs, blog posts and tags pulled live from the database, sensible priorities, and admin/API/checkout correctly excluded. New posts appear without anyone remembering to add them.

SolidNo fake ratings in structured data

The 4.9/5 figure is on the page but never reaches JSON-LD. That restraint is the difference between rewriting a line of copy and recovering from a manual action.

Solid/cards is server-rendered with ISR

Card names, prices and images are in the initial HTML. Googlebot sees a populated page without executing JavaScript — previously it saw an empty shell reading “Loading card designs”.

SolidClean heading structure and honest indexability

Exactly one <h1> on every public page. Transactional and authenticated routes are properly disallowed rather than left to leak into the index.

Do it in this order

Sequenced by return per hour of work. The first three are days, not weeks, and unlock the rest.

  1. Claim and verify Google Business Profile. No code. Largest single lever on local visibility.
  2. Remove the 4.9/5 and 10,000+ claims. One file, minutes. Closes a legal and trust exposure.
  3. Fix the Product schema: correct the prices from the database, and stop emitting it on non-product pages. Clears a live validation failure.
  4. Rewrite all page titles and descriptions with real keywords, price and location. Biggest ranking gain per hour on this list.
  5. Add LocalBusiness schema with complete address, geo and areaServed — after the street address and PIN exist in config.
  6. Add FAQPage and BreadcrumbList, both built from data already on the pages.
  7. Fix product slugs, then build /cards/[slug] with per-card metadata and schema. Slugs first — reordering costs redirects.
  8. Deepen /cards and /about-us toward 1,200+ words of genuinely useful buying content.
  9. Build 5 tier-1 city pages — Chennai, Coimbatore, Madurai, Trichy, Salem — with real local economic context, not a find-and-replace.
  10. Launch the Google Review card line. Highest-converting gap in the catalogue.
  11. Add Tamil for the top pages, with human-written copy only.

Two things to be careful about

Both are traps this category falls into routinely, and both are hard to recover from.

  • Doorway pages. Generating 80 city pages from one template with the name swapped is the classic way to earn a doorway-page action. Every city page needs genuinely different content. Gate it automatically — compare rendered text pairwise and fail the build above ~60% similarity — because it will not be caught by eye at that volume.
  • Rating schema. Do not add aggregateRating until real reviews exist in a system you can point to. Structured-data manual actions are slow and painful to reverse, and the recovery costs far more than the rich result was ever worth.
